Medi-Cal Work Requirements  — tap for details

Enacted · Takes effect Dec 2026
The law
One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21), signed July 4, 2025. Cuts approximately $1 trillion from federal Medicaid spending over 10 years.
What changed
Starting December 31, 2026: Medi-Cal enrollees ages 19–65 who are on the ACA Medicaid expansion must document at least 80 hours per month of qualifying activity — employment, job training, education, or volunteer work — or lose their coverage. There are exemptions for people who are medically fragile, pregnant, caregivers of young children, or already have a serious disability. Paperwork must be filed to claim an exemption.
Who it affects in Menifee
Medi-Cal covers 14.8 million Californians as of mid-2025. The work requirement applies to the Medicaid expansion population — adults without dependent children who gained coverage under the ACA. People already receiving IHSS (in-home care) may face additional paperwork to prove an exemption.
What this means practically If you are on Medi-Cal and between ages 19–65, you will need to document qualifying activities or an exemption by the December 2026 deadline. The most immediate risk is for people who qualify for an exemption but do not know they need to file paperwork to claim it. The same (877) 932-4100 HelpLink line that handles senior services can help connect you with a benefits counselor.

SNAP Food Benefits — Benefit Calculation Change  — tap for details

Enacted · Now in effect
The law
One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21), signed July 4, 2025. The SNAP provisions are projected to reduce total SNAP spending by 36% by 2034 compared to prior projections.
What changed
Under prior law, households receiving LIHEAP (utility assistance) received a higher SNAP benefit because energy assistance was counted as part of their household expenses. The new law changes how energy costs are counted in the SNAP benefit formula, which lowers the monthly benefit for some households. SNAP also added new work requirements that mirror the Medicaid requirements.
Who it affects in Menifee
SNAP recipients who also receive LIHEAP utility assistance will see their monthly SNAP benefit reduced. The size of the reduction depends on household size and benefit level. Separately, adults 18–54 without dependents who were previously exempt from work requirements now have tighter requirements to maintain.
What this means practically If your household uses both SNAP and LIHEAP utility assistance, your SNAP benefit may be lower than it was before July 2025. If you have not already, call (951) 955-4900 (CAP Riverside) to confirm your current LIHEAP enrollment and ask how the SNAP change affects your specific household. The two programs still both exist — the interaction between them changed.

LIHEAP Utility Assistance — Funding at Risk  — tap for details

Watch — Not yet cut
What LIHEAP is
The Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) helps low-income households pay electric and gas bills. In Riverside County, it is administered by Community Action Partnership at (951) 955-4900. Seniors 60 and older are a priority group.
Current status
LIHEAP survived FY2026 intact. Congress funded the program at approximately $4.05 billion for fiscal year 2026 — a small increase — and the remaining funds were fully released to states in April 2026. The program is operating now. However, the Trump administration's FY2027 budget request again proposes eliminating LIHEAP entirely. Federal program staff have not been rehired, so states are running the program without federal training or guidance.
What could happen
Congress has rejected LIHEAP elimination three years in a row, and House Appropriations Committee Chair Tom Cole (R-OK) has said he opposes cutting the program. But the FY2027 appropriations process is not resolved, and funding levels are not guaranteed. The program exists today. Whether it continues past October 2026 depends on Congress.
What this means practically LIHEAP is funded and accepting applications at (951) 955-4900. Apply now if you need utility assistance — the program is operating. The risk is FY2027 (starting October 2026). Congress has blocked elimination before, but apply during this funding period rather than waiting.

SB 417 — Affordable Housing Bond  — tap for details

On November 2026 ballot
What it is
SB 417 (California Affordable Housing Bond Act of 2026) is a $10 billion bond measure that California legislators placed on the November 2026 statewide ballot. Voters will decide whether to approve it.
What the money would fund
$7 billion to the state's Multifamily Housing Program (builds affordable rental units).
$1 billion to the CalHOME program and down payment assistance for first-time buyers.
$800 million to preserve existing affordable housing.
$500 million to acquire and rehabilitate naturally affordable housing.
Who decides
California voters in the November 2026 general election. You must be registered to vote in California to have a say.
Connection to Menifee
Menifee is one of the fastest-growing cities in California and has seen significant housing cost increases. Affordable housing bond funds are distributed statewide through competitive applications by developers and local governments. Menifee would need to apply — the funds do not automatically come here.
What this means practically If you rent in Menifee or have been priced out of homeownership, this bond is relevant to whether affordable units get built here. Your state assembly member (Essayli) and state senator (Seyarto) voted on whether to put this measure on the ballot. Call them to ask where they stand on housing funding.
