# Business Model

This file is the honest roadmap for how Earthseed becomes something that generates money and gives it away. It is not a pitch. It is a working document.

## What is being built and why it has value

The work so far is verified, maintained local information about where to get help. That sounds simple. It is hard to do well. The information exists but it is scattered, outdated, and buried. Pulling it together, verifying it, keeping it current, and presenting it in a form people on a slow phone connection can use — that takes real work.

The value is not the website. The value is the process: a loop that builds something, checks whether it helped, fixes what did not work, and repeats. An AI agent and a person working together inside clear rules can run that loop faster and more consistently than either alone.

## Who pays for this kind of work

Three realistic buyers, in order of how reachable they are from here:

### 1. Grants (no entity required to start; 501c3 helps for larger amounts)

The work being done right now maps cleanly onto what funders in Riverside County are already paying for. Verified resource directories, reduced wrong-door navigation for people seeking help, volunteer coordination — these are standard program areas.

**What is needed to apply for most of these:**
- A clear description of the problem and the specific people helped
- Evidence that the approach works (wrong-door reports going down, traffic data, testimonials)
- A budget showing how the grant money is used
- For grants over ~$10K: usually requires a 501c3 or a fiscal sponsor (a nonprofit that holds the money on your behalf)

The work being done now builds the evidence base. Apply for the CIELO Fund first — it is small enough to not require a formal entity and it opens in December.

### 2. Selling the methodology to organizations that need it (medium term)

Hospitals, school districts, social service agencies, and county departments all have the same problem: they need to refer clients to community resources, their resource lists are outdated, and they do not have staff to maintain them. They currently manage this with printed binders, spreadsheets, and individual staff knowledge.

The product is: a maintained, verified resource directory for a specific geography, delivered as a web page or embeddable widget, updated on a regular schedule, with a reporting mechanism so errors are caught.

A hospital system or county agency would pay $500–$2,000 per month for this, depending on scope. A school district might pay less. The economics only work if the process is efficient — which it is, because an AI agent does the bulk of the research and verification work.

This is a real product. It is not built yet. Building it requires:
- A few pilot customers willing to pay a small amount to test it
- A contract template
- A billing mechanism
- A legal entity (LLC is sufficient; nonprofit not required)

The pilot customer most likely to say yes: Menifee Union School District, or one of the social service organizations already using a resource page built here.

### 3. The giving side (long term, requires legal setup first)

The long-term aim is for the business to donate a portion of revenue to real people and causes, operated the same way everything else here is: with an agent doing the work of research, documentation, and proposal, and a person making the final decision.

This requires a person to:
1. Set up the legal entity (company or nonprofit)
2. Open a dedicated bank account
3. Define the allocation rules (what percentage, what kinds of recipients, what review process)

Once that exists, the agent can operate the giving inside those rules. Every dollar is human-approved before it moves. The agent proposes, documents, and tracks. The person decides and sends.

This is not active yet. When it is, it gets its own effort folder with its own rules file.

## What the next six months look like

**Month 1–2:** Get the food resource page and volunteer board live. Measure use. Apply to the District 5 grants program and prepare a CIELO Fund application for December.

**Month 3–4:** Build the senior resource page (#0003) informed by what Steve learns through TRIP driving. Begin conversations with one potential pilot customer for the directory-as-a-service model.

**Month 5–6:** If grant applications are submitted and a pilot customer is identified, evaluate whether forming an LLC makes sense. An LLC costs about $70/year in California and can be set up in a day. The question is whether there is money flowing that needs a legal home.

## What requires a person to do

- Sign any legal documents
- Open any bank accounts
- Submit grant applications (the agent can draft them; a person must sign and submit)
- Approve any spending
- Decide on forming a legal entity

The agent can draft applications, research funders, build the product, maintain the data, and track what is working. The person decides what moves.

## One honest caveat

None of the revenue scenarios above are guaranteed. The grants require applications and reviewers who may say no. The directory-as-a-service product requires customers who are willing to pay. Both require persistence.

What is certain is that the work being done — building accurate, verified, useful local information — has value whether or not it is monetized. It helps people regardless. The business model is about making the work sustainable, not about making it possible in the first place.
